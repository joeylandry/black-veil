How to use the pages that follow

  • Each survivor has three pages: The Flap (their secret page), then a cut sheet with Cassie's unsigned invitation, the marker and an address label.
  • Cut along the dotted lines. The strip across the top of every page names the guest and marker number, so the pieces can be sorted into five piles.
  • The marker is honoured once, at the bar, by its owner. Its code is in the table above; the Butler types it.
  • Print this page on its own and keep it. Do not leave it on the printer.

THE BLACK VEIL
You are one who got away
Bertrand “Barty” Crane
The Teller
MARKER No. 18

Who you were

You are Bertrand “Barty” Crane, the Black Veil's Teller. You counted the vault's exit advance by hand on 31 October 1924. “Nag Oil” is a name and a gusher you bought with a share of Cassie's gold, and you learned to talk like an oilman because swagger is only arithmetic with an audience.

How you got away

Someone came for you at Hosmer's rooms on Spring Street. You left a body-sized fire in the room, and the search ended there.

Your tell

You count invisible bills with your thumb across your fingertips whenever money, a price or the odds come up. Do it at least three times before the nightcap.

What leaks through

You can count a stack of bills by feel and know any sum to the cent.

The rules for you

  1. You are one who got away. You know the old man hunted you and the people you trusted, and you know who he is. Others probably escaped too, but you do not know how many or who. Everyone else has been told only that survivors are in the room. Your cover is your public card. Keep it.
  2. Your invitation came unsigned, and it knew the new name you have used ever since. Only one person could have known it. Working out who is one of your errands.
  3. You carry marker No. 18. It is yours alone: never trade it, never give it away. It is how you pay the House.
  4. You have told yourself so many stories since 1925 that you are no longer certain of your real reason for coming. You only know that you must be the one. Somebody in this room saw what he did to you; finding out is one of your errands.
  5. The House keeps one bottle of tincture and sells it once a night, to whoever shows the Butler a marker first. Make your offer before the nightcap is served at nine o'clock. Only one of you can be the one.
  6. If the Butler sells to you, the Keeper's nightcap waits in a glass on his table in the Merrimack Room. Pour the vial in unseen, then type the short code printed under the glass's coaster into the nightcap box on this page. Only the buyer's code counts, and the page will say only “Noted.”
  7. Do your tell, a small habit you no longer notice, at least three times before the nightcap. Do not overdo it.
  8. Whoever finishes him takes Cassie's seat and the Veil. You are competing with others you cannot name, and every one of you has the right to try.

Mihir Nagarkatti of Nag Oil,

That is the name you have worn since 1925, and I am one of very few who know what it covers.

The old man will be in the room tonight. You know the one I mean. I will not write his name where a letter can be lost.

Every one of you was meant to be dead, and you are not. Do not waste the evening being surprised.

Why it must be you is yours to remember. You have told yourself a great many stories since. Sit quietly with the true one.

Who you were

You are Edmund Lyle, the Black Veil's Notary. At 23:02 on 31 October 1924 you witnessed the Exit Compact on the private landing, one of four. You have not liked your own signature since.

How you got away

A barker's job was open and nobody checks a barker's papers. You became Luke Shelldon, a man with a megaphone instead of a seal, and you paint every handbill yourself, which is as close to signing things as you dare.

Your tell

You dot every i with a tiny ring and tap your pen twice after you sign anything. Do it at least three times before the nightcap.

Who you were

You are Sylvia Ballard, the Black Veil's Milliner and costume mistress: the masks, the gowns and the hats for the masquerade. On 31 October 1924 you dressed the Veil's people for the Exit. A department store is the one place where nobody asks who made the clothes.

How you got away

You were fitting a gown on Canal Street when the hitman's man came for your girls. You left by the coal cellar with a hatbox and a bolt of silk, and never went back.

Your tell

Who you were

You are Dominic Marek, the Black Veil's Doorman, who held the river door. On the night of the Exit you let two people out at 23:47 and barred the door behind them. You have never sat with your back to a door since.

How you got away

You ran. A gunsmith's shop needed a man who knew doors and locks, and you learned guns because every doorman should know what a bad guest carries.

Your tell

You never sit with your back to a door, and you count the exits in every room you enter. Do it at least three times before the nightcap.

MARKER No. 22

Who you were

You are Winifred “Winnie” Pike, the Black Veil's Runner: the Canadian mail, the late editions, the messages that could not be telephoned. You carried the invitations to the Exit on 31 October 1924.

How you got away

You were on a back road with a message when the hitman's boatman came. You ran, hid under a newsie's bundle, and cried the late edition on a corner until the corner was your own.

Your tell

You glance at the nearest clock every time a door opens, and again when it closes. Do it at least three times before the nightcap.
